# Army-to-Construction Translation Guide

A detailed reference mapping your Army project experience onto construction field engineering. See `content/week1/army-to-construction.json` for the machine-readable source and `#week1` on the site for the interactive version.

## Project #6: NTC Readiness → Mobilization & Site Readiness Planning

**Army context:** Prepared a battalion-sized element for a National Training Center rotation — coordinating logistics, personnel readiness, equipment maintenance, and training timelines against a fixed deployment date.

**Construction equivalent:** Mobilization and site readiness planning — getting a job site, crews, equipment, and permits ready to hit a hard construction start date.

| Army | Construction |
|---|---|
| Readiness inspections | Pre-mobilization site walks and safety plan reviews |
| Equipment maintenance cycles | Equipment/material procurement and staging |
| Personnel certification tracking | Trade certification and site orientation tracking |
| Fixed rotation date | Contractual project start / substantial completion date |

**Key insight:** Both require driving dozens of moving parts toward a fixed, non-negotiable date with no room for slippage.

**How it applies:** You already know how to build a readiness timeline backward from a hard deadline and track every dependency that could slip it — that's exactly what a Field Engineer does when prepping a phase for inspection or turnover.

## Project #8: Multi-Element Coordination → Trade Coordination

**Army context:** Synchronized multiple independent units (infantry, armor, logistics, aviation) operating on the same terrain and timeline, each with different capabilities and reporting chains.

**Construction equivalent:** Synchronizing concrete, MEP, structural steel, and finishes crews — each with different foremen, schedules, and material lead times — working the same building.

| Army | Construction |
|---|---|
| Unit synchronization matrix | Trade sequencing / short-interval schedule |
| Cross-unit liaison | Field Engineer as coordination point between trades |
| Terrain deconfliction | Space/sequence deconfliction |
| Combined arms rehearsal | Pre-installation coordination meeting |

**Key insight:** Multi-element coordination is fundamentally a communication and sequencing discipline — exactly what trade coordination requires.

**How it applies:** You've run the kind of synchronization matrix a Field Engineer builds informally every week: who's on site, in what sequence, and what has to finish before the next trade can start.

## Project #9: Deployment Logistics → Multi-Phase Project Management

**Army context:** Managed a 9-month phased deployment cycle — personnel processing, equipment staging, logistics coordination, and documentation audits — with hard gates between each phase.

**Construction equivalent:** Foundation → structure → MEP rough-in → finishes → inspections → closeout, each phase gated by inspections and sign-offs.

| Army | Construction |
|---|---|
| Personnel processing | Site prep / permitting |
| Equipment staging | Material procurement and delivery scheduling |
| Logistics coordination | Trade scheduling and sequencing |
| Documentation audit | Compliance inspections and sign-offs |

**Key insight:** Both require phased, sequential planning with zero-margin compliance checkpoints.

**How it applies:** You've owned the kind of phase-gate discipline a Field Engineer enforces daily.

## Project #11: Facility Risk Mitigation → Quality Control

**Army context:** Identified and mitigated operational risks across a facility before they became mission-impacting incidents.

**Construction equivalent:** Catching deviations from spec, safety hazards, and coordination conflicts before they cause rework, injury, or schedule slippage.

| Army | Construction |
|---|---|
| Risk register | Punch list / open issues log |
| Preventive maintenance | Pre-installation quality checks |
| Incident root-cause analysis | Defect documentation and corrective action tracking |
| Compliance walk-throughs | Quality/safety inspections |

**Key insight:** Both roles reward the same instinct — catching a small problem early is always cheaper than fixing a big one late.

**How it applies:** Your risk-mitigation mindset is precisely the quality-control instinct JE Dunn wants in a Field Engineer.
